About Me

I'm an Assistant Professor at the Department of Space Science, University of Alabama in Huntsville. I spend most of my time chasing gamma-ray bursts — the brightest explosions in the universe — and thinking about what they can tell us, on their own or together with gravitational waves and high-energy neutrinos. Along the way I've also gotten drawn into active galactic nuclei, magnetar giant flares, and the intergalactic magnetic field. I got my PhD from Eötvös University in Budapest in 2011, then did postdocs at Penn State and George Washington University before joining the faculty here.

Research Interests

  • Gamma-ray burst prompt emission & jet composition
  • Gamma-ray burst polarization
  • Gravitational wave counterparts
  • High-energy neutrinos from gamma-ray bursts
  • GRBs as cosmological probes
  • Magnetar giant flares
  • Intergalactic magnetic fields
  • High-energy emission from active galactic nuclei
  • Spectral & temporal analysis techniques

Current Projects

Fast NICER follow-up for magnetar central engines

What powers a GRB? The central engine is widely thought to be a black hole, but a highly magnetized neutron star (a magnetar) is a viable alternative. In some scenarios, a pulsating magnetar signal would appear in X-rays within an hour of the trigger — we use NICER observations to search for it.
